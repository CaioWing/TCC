A container that can hold water may appear in many consecutive frames of high-resolution aerial video. This study examines how image tile selection affects the mapping of potential \textit{Aedes aegypti} breeding sites, onboard processing demand and energy use per unit area imaged. Two strategies were compared using the YOLO11n detector on six complete flights from the Mosquito Breeding Grounds dataset (MBG-V2), recorded at 40~m. One strategy samples tiles at regular intervals. The other tracks fixed points on the ground and requests a tile when a point enters the image or reaches a quadrant it has yet to visit during that pass. The quadrant-based strategy requested 8,796 tiles across the six flights. For comparison, interval sampling was set to match the number of tiles requested on each flight. The quadrant-based strategy located 56.1\% of the 1,924 annotated containers, compared with 54.8\% for interval sampling. It produced 435 fewer false alarms on the map. For containers detected in the images by both methods, the median time from first appearance to first alert was 1.33~s with quadrant-based selection and 2.37~s with interval sampling. Both selective strategies required 5.59 tiles/s. In a bench test with TensorRT FP16 and decoded images, the Jetson Orin Nano processed 44.87 tiles/s. Processing every tile in every frame located 72.8\% of the containers but would require 719.28 tiles/s to keep up with the camera. At about 16 times the measured throughput, this workload exceeds the capacity of the tested configuration for real-time inference. At 40~m, flight telemetry yielded a median energy use of 239.0~Wh/km$^2$ without the onboard computer. With the 174~g kit active, the estimate rose to 270.3~Wh/km$^2$. At 80~m, the wider swath would reduce this estimate to 172.3~Wh/km$^2$ along the same routes, but the simulated loss of image detail reduced the proportion of containers located by quadrant-based selection to 46.2\%. Over the six flights, quadrant-based selection produced earlier alerts and fewer false alarms for the same estimated detector energy use.

\textbf{Keywords}: \textit{Aedes aegypti}. Unmanned aerial vehicles. Object detection. Onboard computing. Energy efficiency.
