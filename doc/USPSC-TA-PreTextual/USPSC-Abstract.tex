A container that can hold water may appear in dozens of consecutive frames of aerial video. This study examines which image tiles to process when mapping potential \textit{Aedes aegypti} breeding sites and how much energy it would take to carry and operate the detector onboard. Two strategies were compared on six flights recorded at 40~m in the Mosquito Breeding Grounds dataset (MBG-V2). These flights were also used to choose the quadrant-based strategy. This strategy tracks fixed points on the ground and requests a tile when a point enters the image or appears in a new quadrant during the same pass. The other strategy samples frames at regular intervals and sends the same number of tiles per flight. On a workstation, a YOLO11n detector trained on MBG-V2 located 56.1\% of the 1,924 annotated containers with quadrant-based selection and 54.8\% with interval sampling. Quadrant-based selection produced 435 fewer false alarms on the map. For containers detected in the images by both strategies, the median time from first appearance to first alert, measured on the video timeline, was 1.33~s with quadrant-based selection and 2.37~s with interval sampling. Both strategies required an average of 5.59~tiles/s. In a bench test using COCO weights, TensorRT FP16 and decoded images, the Jetson Orin Nano processed 44.87~tiles/s. Processing every tile in every frame raised the share of containers located to 72.8\%, but keeping up with the camera would require about 16 times the measured throughput. At 40~m, flight telemetry indicated a median energy use of 239.0~Wh/km$^2$ without the onboard kit. With the kit active, estimated energy use along the same routes was 270.3~Wh/km$^2$ at 40~m and 172.3~Wh/km$^2$ at 80~m, where the camera would cover a wider swath. When the loss of image detail at 80~m was simulated, quadrant-based selection located only 46.2\% of the containers annotated in the original images.

\textbf{Keywords}: \textit{Aedes aegypti}. Unmanned aerial vehicles. Object detection. Onboard computing. Energy efficiency.
