A container that can hold water may appear in dozens of consecutive frames of aerial video. This study examines which image tiles to process when mapping potential \textit{Aedes aegypti} breeding sites and how much energy it would take to carry and operate the detector onboard. Two strategies were compared using video recordings from six flights at 40~m in the Mosquito Breeding Grounds dataset (MBG-V2). The same recordings informed the choice of the quadrant-based strategy. This strategy tracks fixed points on the ground and requests a tile when a point enters the image or appears in a new quadrant during the same pass. The other strategy samples frames at regular intervals and sends the same number of tiles per flight. On a workstation, a YOLO11n detector trained on MBG-V2 located 56.1\% of the 1,924 annotated containers with quadrant-based selection and 54.8\% with interval sampling. Quadrant-based selection produced 435 fewer false alarms on the map. For containers detected in the images by both strategies, the median time from first annotated appearance to first correct detection in the video was 1.33~s with quadrant-based selection and 2.37~s with interval sampling. Both strategies required an average of 5.59~tiles/s. In a bench test using COCO weights, TensorRT FP16 and decoded images, the Jetson Orin Nano processed 44.87~tiles/s. On the workstation, the detector located 72.8\% of the annotated containers when it processed every tile in every frame. Keeping up with the camera at that rate would require about 16 times the throughput measured on the bench. For the flights at 40~m without the kit, dividing the recorded battery energy by the reconstructed area imaged on each route gave a median of 239.0~Wh/km$^2$. For the same routes, the model estimated a median of 270.3~Wh/km$^2$ with the kit active. Holding each route's estimated energy fixed and recalculating its coverage at 80~m gave a median of 172.3~Wh/km$^2$. When the loss of image detail at 80~m was simulated, quadrant-based selection located only 46.2\% of the containers annotated in the original images.

\textbf{Keywords}: \textit{Aedes aegypti}. Unmanned aerial vehicles. Object detection. Onboard computing. Energy efficiency.
